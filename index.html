01

The casing cracks.

In a landfill, weight, weather, and time crush battery casings. Over years, the protective shell that keeps the chemistry contained begins to fail.[Source: EPA hazardous waste reports]

02

Heavy metals leach.

Rainwater filters through the landfill (creating "leachate") and carries trace metals — zinc, manganese, and in older chemistries cadmium, mercury, and lead — toward soil and groundwater.[Source: EPA leachate studies]

03

Fires get sparked.

Lithium and lithium-ion batteries in trash streams have been linked to a rise in waste-facility fires across the U.S. and Europe. Improperly discarded batteries are now one of the fastest-growing fire risks at material recovery facilities.[Source: Reuters / EPA reporting]

Note on numbers: industry and government estimates vary. We use the most widely cited figures and link to sources rather than pretend we have one tidy number. The trend, across every estimate, is the same direction.

10 / FAQ

Honest answers, sources where we have them.

Is it actually true that one Paleblue replaces 1,000 disposables?
The number comes from cycle life: each Paleblue is engineered for 1,000+ full recharge cycles. Each cycle delivers comparable runtime to a fresh disposable, so the displacement math is roughly one-to-one. Real-world results depend on the device, care, and how deeply you cycle the battery — but 1,000+ is the design spec, not marketing exaggeration.[Source: Paleblue specs]
What's wrong with regular disposable batteries?
The chemistry inside an alkaline battery isn't acutely dangerous in your junk drawer — but at scale, it adds up. Roughly 10 million disposables are tossed per day in the U.S.; estimates suggest about 85% end up in landfills. Over time, casings break down, and trace heavy metals (zinc, manganese, and in older chemistries cadmium or mercury) can leach into landfill leachate. Modern lithium-ion disposables also drive a measurable rise in fires at waste-handling facilities.[Source: EPA / Call2Recycle / Reuters]
Do Paleblue batteries work in every device that takes alkalines?
Almost all of them. Paleblue batteries deliver standard 1.5V (or 3V for CR123A) and work in flashlights, remotes, cameras, smart locks, toys, headlamps, and most general electronics. As always, check the device manual — a handful of very-low-drain devices may specify alkaline or a specific battery type.
How long do they take to charge?
Charge times vary by size. CR123A fully charges in about 100 minutes. D batteries take about 6.9 hours. AA and AAA fall in between. All charge with the included USB-C cable from any standard USB-C source — laptop, phone charger, car, or power bank.[Source: Paleblue specs]
What do I do when a Paleblue reaches end of life?
Don't trash it. Lithium-ion batteries are recyclable, and the materials inside (lithium, cobalt, nickel, copper) are valuable enough that proper recycling recovers a large share. Most major hardware stores accept household batteries for free recycling. Call2Recycle's locator can find a drop-off near you in 30 seconds.[Source: Call2Recycle]
Are they safe? What about overcharging or fires?
Every Paleblue battery includes built-in protection circuitry against overcharging, over-discharge, over-temperature, and short-circuit. As with any lithium-ion battery: charge with standard USB-C sources, don't store at extreme temperatures, and stop using a cell that shows physical damage or unusual heating.
What's your return policy?
30-day returns on any order. Limited lifetime warranty covers manufacturing defects and normal-use failures. Free shipping on orders over $50.
